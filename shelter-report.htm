Section 01
Overview

This project looks at two years of animal shelter data from 54 U.S. states and territories. The dashboard tracks dogs and cats from the moment they enter a shelter until they leave. By tracking adoptions, euthanasia numbers, and stray animal trends, this tool helps rescue teams see where help is needed most and send resources to those areas.

Section 02
Business Problem

Animal shelters across the country handle millions of pets every year, but their records are scattered. Because the files are hidden in different systems, it is hard for managers to see which locations are running out of space or find big trends across years. Without a clear dashboard, teams cannot see serious problems—like the 2.3% rise in cat euthanasia—or help shelters in the southern states that are struggling the most.

Section 03
Methodology

The dashboard was built using three clear steps:
• Data Cleaning: Collected raw files from multi-year shelter reports. Removed extra text and commas from the data, and changed everything into clean numbers so calculations wouldn't break.
• Grouping Trends: Organised the locations into matching pairs so we could accurately compare 2024 numbers directly against 2025 numbers.
• Simple Formulas:
  - The Euthanasia Rate is calculated as: Total Euthanasia divided by Total Intakes, multiplied by 100 for each state.
  - The Average Adoption Rate is calculated as: Total Adoptions divided by Total Intakes, multiplied by 100, then averaged across all states.

Section 04
Key Insights
📉

Dog Euthanasia is Dropping

Dog euthanasia dropped by 6.8% across the country from 2024 to 2025 (from 343K down to 320K). At the same time, the dog adoption rate grew from 56.4% to 58.1% due to better community outreach.

⚠️

Cat Pressure is Growing

The number of cats entering shelters rose by 0.6%, but cat euthanasia climbed by 2.3%. This shows that shelters are struggling to handle the growing cat population even though cat adoptions are up (+2.2%).

📍

High Activity in Two States

California and Texas alone make up about 26% of all dog intakes in the country. Louisiana, New Mexico, and Alabama have the highest euthanasia rates, showing that southern shelters need more resources.

Section 07
Conclusion

In summary, organizing the data lists reveals very different trends for dogs and cats. While dog adoptions went up and euthanasia rates dropped by 6.8%, cat shelters faced a lot of pressure due to higher intake numbers. To balance these trends, future rescue programs should focus extra help on high-volume states like California and Texas, while building targeted cat adoption plans in southern territories.
