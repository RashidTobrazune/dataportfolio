Section 01
Overview

This project is an interactive, four-act logistics review dashboard built to analyze a fleet of 48 commercial delivery vehicles and 36 drivers across one year on the road. The report tells a clear operational story: how much money the fleet made and spent (Act I: The Fleet Pulse), where delays and lost hours are concentrated (Act II: Friction Points), which vehicle types and drivers run most efficiently (Act III: Driving Efficiency), and exactly what three actions management should take to recover lost profits (Act IV: What We Do Next).

Section 02
Business Problem

Over the year, the fleet earned $4.74M but spent $3.82M, leaving an operating profit of 19%. While 86% of deliveries arrived on time, operational hiccups quietly drained $156K from the bottom line—equivalent to 17% of total company profit. Late deliveries, canceled runs, and overdue maintenance were burning unnecessary fuel and wasting labor hours. Dispatchers and fleet managers had no easy way to see which routes suffered the worst friction, which vehicles earned the best margins, or how much money could be saved with targeted operational fixes.

Section 03
Methodology

The analytics pipeline was built in three clear steps:
• Trip & Telematics Integration: Combined GPS trip logs, fuel consumption records, vehicle maintenance tickets, and driver shift times across 7,110 completed and scheduled runs.
• Cost Leak Categorization: Quantified financial losses into three plain buckets: profit lost on late trips ($108K / 69%), money spent on canceled runs that earned zero ($25K / 16%), and excess fuel burned by overdue-service vehicles ($24K / 15%).
• Actionable Opportunity Modeling: Formulated three realistic fixes with verified dollar values: servicing 17 vehicles marked Due Soon or Overdue (worth ~$29K/yr), cutting late trips by 25% on worst routes (worth ~$27K/yr), and pairing junior drivers with senior mentors for 3 months (worth ~$35K/yr).

Section 04
Key Insights
⏱️

Late Trips Are the Biggest Money Drain

Late trips accounted for 944 deliveries and cost the company $108K in lost profit (69% of all lost money). The East region suffered the most delays (15.5% late trips), with South semi-trucks peaking at 18.9%.

🚐

Delivery Vans Keep the Most Profit

Delivery Vans achieved the highest profit margin at 30.1% ($155K profit on $515K earned). In contrast, large Semi-Trucks generated the highest revenue ($3.01M) but kept only 15.9% in net profit.

🔧

Overdue Maintenance Burns 12% More Fuel

Vehicles with overdue service dropped in fuel efficiency score from 1.03 (Good) down to 0.89 (Overdue). Skipping regular service directly burns 12% more fuel and triggers avoidable trip cancellations.

Section 07
Conclusion

In summary, organizing fleet operations into a four-act dashboard uncovered exactly where $156K in annual profit was leaking away. Instead of generic advice, the data points to three clear, high-return moves: service the 17 overdue vehicles immediately to save $29K in wasted fuel, add buffer time to chronic long-haul routes like Houston → Atlanta to cut delay costs by $27K, and pair junior drivers (who average 17% late trips vs 12% for others) with senior mentors for 3 months to save another $35K. Together, these three steps can win back $91K every year—boosting company profits by roughly 10%.
