Executive Summary

This case study analyzes workforce demographics, promotion cycles, work-life balance scores, and attrition indicators across four key enterprise branches. The dashboard reveals critical insights regarding performance tracking disparities, identifying a distinct 32.85% attrition concentration risk within specific high-spending salary segments and technical functions, while highlighting critical opportunities to enhance gender diversification within senior executive pipelines.

Analysis
Key Insights & Strategic Findings
📉

Sales Volume Pressures Exits

The Sales division experiences the highest voluntary turnover rate at 20.6% regardless of gender. Cross-referencing monthly metrics shows a strong correlation with job roles requiring extended overtime targets.

⚠️

High-Spending Attrition Target

Data segments with higher salary ranges indicate an isolated 32.85% churn spike. Exit surveys mapped in Power BI emphasize competitive market poaching as a key driver for mid-level leaders.

📍

Executive Pipeline Diversity Gap

While the overall firm maintains a healthy 41.8% female baseline, the senior executive leadership team dips significantly to 28.1%, identifying a target area for corporate mentorship frameworks.

⚖️

Work-Life Balance Safeguard

Staff recording a Work-Life Balance index rating of 3.0 or greater out of 4 demonstrated an attrition risk below 8.2%, proving flexible work policies directly prevent team burnout.

🔄

Overtime Correlation Confirmed

A stark breakdown shows employees tasked with consistent overtime hours possess a 3x higher probability of voluntary departure compared to peers with fixed operational schedules.

🏆

Stability Found in R&D Core

The R&D sector anchors company operational stability with a standout 87.1% combined retention pace. This highlights the successful onboarding structures currently deployed within technical divisions.

Methodology & Technical Notes

Data Source: Internal HR Workforce Matrix Database — Anonymized historical profiles tracking 1,470 unique employee trajectories across 4 specialized branches.

Scope: The analysis establishes metrics on performance distribution, promotion cycles, and time-in-role vectors. Data formatting, cleaning, and null handling were handled via Power Query before modeling the relationships in Power BI.

Attrition Calculation: Formulated as (Total Exited Staff ÷ Total Hired Records within Segment) × 100. Retention Index: Represents active headcount as a share of baseline targets, tracked sequentially across business cycles.

Limitations: Survey metrics (e.g., Work-Life Balance scores) rely entirely on voluntary self-reporting. Exit analysis omits seasonal contractor roles to keep core full-time workforce trends accurate.
