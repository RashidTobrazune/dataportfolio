Section 01
Overview

This project is an interactive data storytelling dashboard built to review the real-world financial and operational performance of a 60-store coffee chain across 12 UK regions. Instead of showing plain numbers, the system tells a four-part story: why busy stores are not always the most profitable, whether customer loyalty apps pay for themselves, which drinks actually make the money, and how adjusting weekend staff hours can save hundreds of thousands of pounds each year.

Section 02
Business Problem

Every store in the chain sells essentially the same coffee at the same prices, and stores across different regions bring in roughly the same sales (about £62k per month, differing by only 8%). Yet, their final profits were wildly different: stores in Northern Ireland kept 28p of every £1 taken in, while stores in London kept just 8p. Management needed a clear tool to see why high-sales stores were struggling, whether customer reward programs were actually making money, and where to safely expand or cut costs in 2025.

Section 03
Methodology

The analysis was conducted in three straightforward steps:
• Cost Breakdown: Split each store's incoming revenue into four clear buckets: Cost of Goods (what the coffee ingredients cost), Staff Wages, Shop Rent, and App Rewards.
• Story-Driven Interactive Modeling: Built dynamic narrative pages that test hypotheses—like comparing rent costs against customer traffic (finding a near-zero correlation of 0.15), and comparing app user share against store payback (correlation of 0.76).
• Region Scoring System: Ranked every region on a 100-point scale based on four simple business rules: Profit Margin (40%), Sales Growth (25%), Consistency / Few Loss Months (20%), and Sales per Square Foot (15%).

Section 04
Key Insights
🏢

Rent Decides the Profit

High sales do not equal high profit. London stores pay 19p of every £1 in rent and keep just 8p in profit, whereas Northern Ireland pays only 7p in rent and keeps 28p. Paying higher rent does not bring more customers.

📱

App Rewards Lose Money in Slow Stores

Across the chain, rewards cost £1.7M while members generated only £1.6M in extra sales, losing 10p on every £1 given out. Stores need over 40% app adoption for rewards to break even.

☕

Filter Coffee Is the Secret Winner

Espresso brings in the most cash (46% of sales), but simple filter coffee is the best earner—it keeps 88p of every £1 taken in (compared to 84p for espresso and 74p for food).

Section 07
Conclusion

In summary, turning complex retail sales logs into clear visual stories revealed four high-impact actions for the business: open new stores only where rent is low (like Wales and Northern Ireland), fix or pause the mobile reward app in branches where adoption is under 40%, promote high-margin filter coffee across marketing menus, and reduce weekend staffing by ~10 hours per store to unlock £986k in annual cost savings.
