Section 01
Overview

This project is an interactive, multi-page dashboard built to track student success, student retention, and donation metrics for an educational non-profit organization. The system connects enrollment numbers, graduation data, test pass rates, and fundraising campaigns. This gives managers a clear view of how different school departments are performing alongside the organization's financial health.

Section 02
Business Problem

Before this dashboard was built, tracking school performance and donation campaigns was frustrating. Student grades and graduation rates were kept in separate files from fundraising records. Because these files were disconnected, managers could not easily see which departments needed extra academic support, how many students stayed enrolled semester-over-semester, or why total donations dropped slightly by 3.65% between years.

Section 03
Methodology

The project was built using three simple steps:
• Data Cleaning & Transformation: Merged raw student academic records with non-profit donation databases. Used Power Query to fix formatting errors, remove empty fields, and separate student levels cleanly into undergraduate and graduate groups.
• Cross-Filtering Setup: Connected the data models so filters for specific years, quarters, and departments update all charts across academic performance and fundraising at the same time.
• Simple Calculations:
  - The Graduation Rate is calculated as: Total Graduates divided by Total Enrolled Students within that cohort, multiplied by 100.
  - The Student Retention Rate is calculated as: Total Retained Students divided by Total Prior Cohort Students, multiplied by 100.

Section 04
Key Insights
🎓

High Pass Rates in Business

The Business department led the school with an 83.5% certification pass rate, even though the Arts department held the highest overall student grade point average at 83.0%.

💰

Event Channel Drives Donations

Fundraising events proved to be the most powerful donor channel, bringing in 34.39% ($268K) of total funding, followed closely by website donations at 31.15% ($242K).

📉

Minor Drop in Funding

Total fundraising experienced a small drop of 3.65% compared to the previous year. The charts pinpoint this drop to lower engagement in later campaign waves, showing exactly where to adjust next year.

Section 07
Conclusion

In summary, combining educational data with donation tracking inside one dashboard gives managers complete clarity over operations. While student academic metrics and certification pass rates look great across departments like Business and Arts, fundraising requires minor adjustments to reverse the 3.65% yearly drop. Future campaigns can shift focus from lower-performing channels toward highly interactive live events and website donation flows.
